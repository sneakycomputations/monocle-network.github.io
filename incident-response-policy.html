Incident Response Policy

This policy defines how Monocle Network responds to security incidents, exploits, and other critical events that affect peers, infrastructure, or network operations. It establishes the process from initial detection through to post-incident review and peer compensation.

1. Overview

Monocle Network takes security seriously. While Monocle does not develop the Haveno software, it is responsible for operating the network, protecting peers, and responding to incidents that affect the community.

This policy ensures that when something goes wrong, Monocle responds promptly, transparently, and with peer protection as the top priority.

2. What Constitutes an Incident

An incident is any event that compromises or threatens the security, integrity, availability, or trustworthiness of Monocle Network. Incidents are categorized by type and severity.

2.1 Incident Types

Type Description Examples
Protocol exploit A vulnerability in the Haveno software being actively exploited Trade deposit theft, protocol manipulation, arbitrator bypass
Infrastructure incident Failure or compromise of Monocle-operated infrastructure Node compromise, key exposure, DDoS causing sustained outage
Operational incident Misconduct or failure in Monocle's operational processes Arbitrator corruption, contributor fraud, key mismanagement
Financial incident Anomaly or compromise affecting network fee or reserve wallets Unauthorized transaction from reserve wallet, fee collection discrepancy
Privacy incident Unintended exposure of peer or contributor data Information leak, deanonymization event, log exposure

2.2 Severity Levels

Severity Definition Response
Critical Active exploitation with peers losing funds or privacy compromised at scale Immediate network pause, emergency response
High Confirmed vulnerability with significant potential impact but limited current exploitation Urgent response, potential partial pause
Medium Vulnerability or incident with limited scope, requires action but no immediate peer harm Prioritized response, continued operations
Low Minor issue with minimal impact, does not require emergency response Routine fix, normal cadence

3. Incident Response Roles

Monocle Network is responsible for monitoring for potential incidents, declaring incidents when warranted, and approving major decisions such as a network pause or Peer Security Reserve activation. The following roles support the response:

3.1 Incident Lead

Upon declaration of a Critical or High severity incident, an Incident Lead is designated. Responsibilities:

  • Coordinate the overall response
  • Make operational decisions (pause, resume, communication timing)
  • Authorize Peer Security Reserve activation
  • Ensure this policy is followed

The Incident Lead is designated by Monocle Network. If a designation cannot be made quickly, the first to declare the incident serves as Incident Lead until a designation is made.

3.2 Contributors

Contributors may be called upon to:

  • Provide operational support during the incident (e.g., node operators, support contributors)
  • Assist with peer communication and support
  • Provide technical expertise relevant to the incident

3.3 Arbitrators

Arbitrators may be involved when:

  • A trade dispute is related to the incident
  • Protocol-level actions are needed to resolve trades affected by the incident

4. Incident Response Process

4.1 Phase 1 - Detection and Declaration

StepActionTimeline
1.1Potential incident detected (via monitoring, peer report, responsible disclosure, or contributor observation)—
1.2Monocle Network assesses initial severity and scopeWithin 2 hours
1.3If Critical or High: incident declared, Incident Lead designatedWithin 4 hours
1.4If Medium or Low: logged and tracked, normal response process beginsWithin 24 hours

4.2 Phase 2 - Containment

For Critical and High severity incidents:

StepActionTimeline
2.1Incident Lead determines whether to pause network operationsImmediately after declaration
2.2If pause is warranted: network operations are suspended to prevent further harmWithin 1 hour of decision
2.3Root cause is isolated to the extent possibleWithin 12 hours
2.4Scope of impact is assessed - how many peers are affected, what funds are at riskWithin 24 hours

Network Pause Decision

A network pause is the most significant action Monocle can take. It should be invoked when:

  • Peers are actively losing funds and the mechanism is not yet understood
  • Continuing operations would expose more peers to harm
  • The integrity of the trade protocol cannot be assured

During a pause:

  • No new trades can be initiated
  • Existing in-progress trades are held in their current state
  • Peers are notified through all community channels
  • The reason for the pause is communicated honestly and promptly

4.3 Phase 3 - Notification

StepActionTimeline
3.1Community notification posted to all channels (SimpleX, Matrix, forum)Within 6 hours of declaration
3.2Detailed incident notice published on GitHubWithin 24 hours
3.3Affected peers contacted directly where possibleWithin 48 hours

Communication Principles

During an incident, Monocle Network will:

  • Communicate promptly - Even partial information is better than silence
  • Be honest - What is known, what is unknown, and what is being investigated will be stated
  • Avoid speculation - Causes, scope, and timelines will not be guessed at until confirmed
  • Provide updates - Regular updates at minimum every 12 hours for Critical incidents and every 24 hours for High incidents
  • Respect privacy - Affected peers will not be identified publicly without their consent

What to Communicate

InformationInitial NoticeSubsequent Updates
Incident typeYesYes
Severity levelYesYes
Whether operations are pausedYesYes
Scope of impact (number of peers, funds at risk)If knownAs assessment progresses
Recommended actions for peersYesAs needed
Estimated timeline for resolutionIf knownAs assessment progresses
Specific technical details of the vulnerabilityNo - until remediatedAfter remediation

Technical details of the vulnerability will not be disclosed until remediation is in place, to prevent copycat exploitation.

4.4 Phase 4 - Remediation

StepActionTimeline
4.1Develop or coordinate a fix for the vulnerabilityAs soon as possible
4.2If the fix is in Haveno software: coordinate with Haveno contributors, fund emergency bounty if neededAs soon as possible
4.3If the fix is in Monocle infrastructure: implement directlyAs soon as possible
4.4Test the fix and verify it addresses the root causeBefore resuming operations
4.5Resume network operations once the fix is confirmedAfter verification

Resuming Operations

The decision to resume operations requires:

  • The root cause has been identified and remediated
  • The fix has been tested and verified
  • Those coordinating the response agree that resuming is safe (unanimous for Critical, majority for High)
  • A resumption notice is published to all community channels at least 2 hours before operations resume

4.5 Phase 5 - Loss Assessment and Public Disclosure

StepActionTimeline
5.1Identify all affected trades and assess total lossesWithin 7 days
5.2Cross-reference with trade records, arbitrator reports, and peer submissionsWithin 7 days
5.3Public disclosure of the total value of losses, the number of affected peers, and the number of impacted tradesWithin 14 days
5.4Open a window for affected peers to submit a Monero address for compensation and any supplementary proof of lossWithin 30 days following public disclosure

Loss Assessment Methodology

  • Losses are assessed based on verified evidence - trade records, on-chain transactions, and arbitrator determinations
  • Only direct losses caused by the incident are eligible for compensation - indirect or consequential losses are not covered
  • The loss assessment report is published on GitHub, anonymized - no peer identifiers
  • If the total of verified losses exceeds the available Peer Security Reserve balance, compensation is distributed on a pro-rata basis (see Section 5)

4.6 Phase 6 - Compensation

StepActionTimeline
6.1Publish a table listing each affected trade, the amount lost, and the compensation addressWithin 10 days after the compensation submission deadline
6.2Process compensation distributions to the addresses providedWithin 10 days after publication of the table
6.3Publish all addresses, transaction IDs and secret transaction keys for independent verificationAt time of distribution
6.4Notify compensated peersAt time of distribution

Fraudulent claims - where a claimant is determined to have caused or participated in the exploit - are rejected, with the reasoning published.

5. Peer Security Reserve Activation

The Peer Security Reserve is Monocle's primary mechanism for reimbursing peers affected by exploits. Activation follows the process defined in the Reserve Allocation Policy.

5.1 Activation Triggers

The Peer Security Reserve is activated when:

  • A protocol exploit has resulted in verified peer losses
  • An infrastructure incident has caused direct financial harm to peers

5.2 Reserve Cascade

If the Peer Security Reserve balance is insufficient, the balances of the Haveno Network Development Grant and the Open Source Sustainability Grant will be redirected to peer compensation.

5.3 Pro-Rata Distribution

If total verified losses exceed total available reserve funds, each affected peer receives an equal proportion of their loss:

Compensation per peer = (Available reserve funds ÷ Total verified losses) × Individual verified loss

Monocle does not guarantee full reimbursement.

5.4 Compensation is Not a Guarantee

The Peer Security Reserve is funded from network fees. Its balance depends entirely on trade volume and the time since the last incident. Monocle commits to distributing all available reserve funds to affected peers but cannot guarantee that losses will be fully covered.

6. Responsible Disclosure Integration

If a security incident results from a vulnerability reported through the Responsible Disclosure Policy:

  • The reporter may be eligible for a bounty, paid from the Peer Security Reserve
  • Bounty payment and peer compensation are separate processes - a bounty does not reduce the funds available for peer compensation
  • The reporter's identity remains confidential unless they choose to disclose it
  • The reporter is not held responsible for any exploitation of the vulnerability by third parties, provided they adhered to the Responsible Disclosure Policy

7. Infrastructure Incidents

7.1 Node Failures

If a Monocle-operated node (Monero node, price node, seed node) fails:

  • The node operator is notified immediately
  • Redundancy should prevent any single node failure from affecting network operations
  • Node failures are not eligible for Peer Security Reserve compensation - they affect availability, not peer funds

7.2 Key Compromise

If a private key for a network fee or reserve wallet is compromised:

  1. All funds are immediately transferred to a new wallet
  2. The new wallet address and view key are published
  3. A full incident review is conducted
  4. Wallet management practices are reviewed and updated if necessary

7.3 Sustained DDoS

If Monocle infrastructure is subject to a sustained DDoS attack:

  • Node operators implement DDoS protection measures
  • If the attack prevents peers from accessing the network for more than 24 hours, Monocle Network assesses whether a temporary pause is warranted
  • Community notification is posted with expected recovery timeline
  • DDoS incidents do not trigger Peer Security Reserve activation unless they are used as cover for a concurrent exploit

8. Operational Incidents

8.1 Arbitrator Misconduct

If an arbitrator is found to have acted corruptly or in bad faith:

  1. The arbitrator is suspended immediately pending investigation
  2. All trades decided by the arbitrator during the suspect period are reviewed
  3. Affected peers are identified and, if financially harmed, compensation is considered from the Peer Security Reserve
  4. The arbitrator's access is revoked permanently
  5. A post-incident review is published

8.2 Contributor Misconduct

If a contributor violates the Contributor Code of Conduct in a way that affects peers:

  1. The contributor is suspended from their role
  2. The scope of impact is assessed
  3. If peer harm is confirmed, the response follows the same process as a security incident
  4. The Contributor Code of Conduct consequences are applied

9. Post-Incident Review

Every Critical and High severity incident, and any incident that results in peer compensation, requires a post-incident review.

9.1 Review Contents

The review is published on GitHub and includes:

SectionContent
Incident summaryType, severity, timeline of events
Root causeWhat caused the incident and how it was exploited or triggered
Impact assessmentNumber of affected peers, total losses, reserve funds used
Response timelineKey decisions and when they were made
What went wellEffective aspects of the response
What could be improvedGaps, delays, or failures in the response
RemediationWhat was done to fix the vulnerability or issue
Policy updatesAny changes to policies resulting from the incident

9.2 Review Timeline

  • Draft review completed within 30 days of incident resolution
  • Review published on GitHub within 45 days of incident resolution
  • Community feedback on the review is welcomed for 14 days after publication

9.3 No Blame for Reporters

If the incident was reported through responsible disclosure, the post-incident review will not identify the reporter without their explicit consent. The review focuses on the vulnerability and the response, not on who found it.
