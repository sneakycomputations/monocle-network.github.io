Support Policy

This policy outlines the support available to peers, the standards governing support contributors, and the expectations for peers seeking assistance.

Part 1: Purpose and Scope

1.1 Purpose

This policy defines how support is provided to peers on Monocle Network, what peers can expect, and what is expected of them.

1.2 Scope

Support covers questions and issues related to Monocle Network, including:

  • Using the Monocle Network application
  • Understanding trading rules and processes
  • Navigating the dispute resolution process
  • Wallet issues within the Monocle Network application
  • Account and configuration questions

Support is available to all peers at no cost.

1.3 What Support Is Not

Support does not cover:

  • Disputes about specific trades. Peers who need help with a specific trade should start the dispute resolution process
  • Legal, financial, or tax advice. Support contributors will not provide guidance on legal matters, financial decisions, or tax obligations
  • External service issues. Problems with hardware wallets, operating systems, Tor connectivity, or internet service providers may be better directed to those services. Support contributors and community members may be able to point peers to appropriate resources, but cannot resolve external issues

1.4 Community Support

In Monocle Network community spaces, anyone can provide support and offer help. There are also Support Contributors whose designated role is to offer support. Both community members and Support Contributors may respond to support requests.

1.5 Self-Service Resources

The Monocle Network wiki is available for peers seeking documentation, guides, and answers to common questions. Peers are encouraged to check the wiki before requesting support. Support contributors may include links to relevant wiki pages in their responses.

Part 2: Support Channels

2.1 Available Channels

Support is provided mainly through the SimpleX Support group.

The following are also supported to expand Monocle's reach:

  • Matrix - Community support and discussion
  • GitHub - Bug reports, feature requests, and code-related issues
  • Community Forum - Longer-form questions and community discussion
  • Telegram - Community support and discussion
  • Reddit - Community support and discussion

2.2 Channel-Specific Guidelines

  • SimpleX offers the fastest response times and supports private chats for sharing identifying information (see Section 6.2)
  • GitHub is the appropriate channel for bug reports and feature requests
  • Forum, Matrix, Telegram, and Reddit are public community channels where peers can ask questions and receive help from both community members and Support Contributors

2.3 Language

Support is conducted in English. Support Contributors who speak other languages may use those languages in private chats to assist peers, but English is the primary language across all channels.

Part 3: What Peers Can Expect from Support

3.1 Responsiveness

  • SimpleX: often within 3 hours
  • all other channels: up to 24 hours

Community members providing help on public channels respond at their own discretion.

3.2 Competence and Knowledge

Support Contributors will possess the necessary knowledge to assist with Monocle Network-related questions. Support Contributors unfamiliar with specific aspects of an issue will seek to gain understanding or consult resources before responding.

3.3 Fairness and Impartiality

Support Contributors will act fairly and impartially. No peer will be given undue advantage or preference in receiving support.

3.4 Timezone Coverage

Support Contributors are located across different time zones. This provides broad coverage, meaning peers can often reach someone regardless of when they need help. However, if a peer wants to speak with a specific Support Contributor, they should take into account that the contributor may be in a different timezone and may not be available immediately.

3.5 Informal Resolution

Support interactions are informal and text-based. They come to a natural conclusion when an issue is resolved, or when a peer goes to try a suggested solution before reporting back. There is no formal closure process.

3.6 No Charge

Support is provided at no charge to peers.

Part 4: What Support Expects from Peers

4.1 Respectful Communication

Peers will keep all support interactions friendly and respectful. Harassment, abusive language, or hostile behaviour is not acceptable.

4.2 Awareness of Available Resources

Peers are encouraged to check the Monocle Network wiki and existing community discussions before requesting support, as many common questions are already addressed there.

4.3 Provision of Information

Support Contributors may request the following information to assist with an issue:

  • Operating system
  • Version number of the Monocle Network application
  • Error message information

Identifying information, such as onion addresses, trade IDs, and transaction IDs, must only be shared in private chats, never in public channels.

4.4 Response Time

Peers should respond to follow-up questions from Support Contributors in a timely manner. Delays in responding may result in the support interaction concluding before the issue is resolved.

Part 5: Escalation

5.1 Escalation to Dispute Resolution

If a peer needs help with a specific trade that is in dispute, they should start the dispute resolution process. Support Contributors cannot assist with specific trade disputes.

5.2 Escalation of Systemic Issues

If Support Contributors identify a systemic issue (multiple peers reporting the same problem), they will contact other contributors to assess whether the issue requires network-level action.

5.3 Escalation of Security Issues

If a support interaction reveals a potential security vulnerability, Support Contributors will direct the peer to the Responsible Disclosure Policy and ensure the matter is handled through the appropriate channel.

5.4 Feedback and Dissatisfaction

If a peer is dissatisfied with the support they received, they can:

  • Seek help from a different Support Contributor or community member, anyone can provide support
  • Provide feedback on the platform where the support interaction occurred
  • Post on GitHub repositories or the community forum to escalate the concern
  • Escalate the issue as set out in the Contributor Code of Conduct.

Part 6: Confidentiality, Privacy, and Safety

6.1 Information Handling

Support interactions in public channels are accessible to existing and new peers. No separate logs are kept. Peers should assume that anything shared in a public channel is publicly visible.

6.2 Sensitive Information

Identifying information such as onion addresses, trade IDs, transaction IDs, must only be shared in private chats, never in public channels.

6.3 What Support Will Never Ask For

Support Contributors and community members providing support will never ask for:

  • Names or real-world identities
  • Physical addresses
  • Email addresses
  • Passwords
  • Seed words

6.4 Scam Awareness

Scammers are active in cryptocurrency support channels looking for people to target. Peers should exercise caution at all times:

  • Except on SimpleX, Support Contributors will never DM you first. While SimpleX guarantees that DMs you receive can only come from group admins, if someone messages you privately on any other platform, assume it is a scam attempt
  • No one legitimately needs your seed words. Never share your seed words with anyone for any reason
  • No one legitimately will send you a link to enter your seed words. If someone sends you a link and asks you to enter your seed, it is a scam
  • Outside of SimpleX, ignore unsolicited private messages about support issues from anyone you did not explicitly contact

If you encounter a suspected scammer, report them in the public support channel.
