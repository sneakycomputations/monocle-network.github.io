# Peer Security Reserve

***In the event of any exploit affecting peers' funds, all available XMR in the reserves will be directed toward compensating affected peers***

Safety of the peers is Monocle's top priority, and in the event of an exploit the balances of all reserves will be directed toward compensating affected peers.

While Monocle is not directly liable for losses resulting from the underlying Haveno protocol, the network voluntarily maintains reserves to mitigate the impact of potential exploits. This commitment reflects the network's dedication to peers' safety and the continued development of the Haveno ecosystem.

## Peer Protection Measures

Monocle operates an instance of the Haveno network, but makes no claims about the inherent safety of the Haveno protocol itself. 

However, Monocle allocates 33% of collected network fees to Community Reserves, which include Peer Security Reserve, Haveno Network Development Grant, and Open Source Sustainability Grant. In the event of an exploit, **all available balances** across these reserves are directed toward compensating affected peers.

Initially, reserve balances will be modest, but during that period network volume is also expected to be low, limiting potential exposure. As network activity grows, reserve balances scale proportionally with fee collection.

## Scope of Peer Security Reserve

Peer Security Reserve is used for:

* Compensating peers affected by protocol exploits
* Compensating reporters for responsibly disclosed vulnerabilities approved as part of the [Responsible Disclosure Policy](https://)

## Non-Eligible Uses of Peer Security Reserve

Peer Security Reserve will not compensate peers in case of:

* User error
* User data loss
* Bank chargebacks
* Phishing
* Social engineering targeting individual peers (e.g., attackers pretending to be support contributors)
* [Arbitration appeals](https://) - these are funded from the network fees
* Sybil attacks, self-dealing, and fraudulent claims involving controlled accounts

## Definition of Exploit

An **exploit** is a vulnerability in the Monocle Network protocol, the underlying Haveno codebase, or associated infrastructure, that is used to compromise the integrity of trades, divert funds from their intended recipients, or otherwise cause financial loss to peers through no fault of their own. 

## Exploit Response Procedure

1. Network operations will be paused until the exploit has been patched.
2. Within 24 hours the community will be notified of the exploit through all available channels.
3. Within 7 days affected trades will be identified and total losses assessed.
4. Within 14 days the total value of losses, number of affected peers, and number of impacted trades will be publicly disclosed.
5. Within 30 days following public disclosure, affected peers will submit a Monero address for compensation and any supplementary proof of loss.
6. Within 10 days after the compensation submission deadline, a table listing each affected trade, the amount lost, and the compensation address will be published.
7. Within 10 days after publication, compensation distributions will be processed to the addresses provided.
8. All addresses, transaction IDs and keys will be published for independent verification.

Claim eligibility and proof requirements vary depending on the nature of the exploit. Specific requirements will be communicated at the time of public disclosure.

Cases will be reviewed by contributors. If a claimant is determined to have caused or participated in the exploit, their claim will be rejected and the reasoning for that decision will be provided. Such determinations require consensus of contributors.

If a peer provides an invalid or undeliverable address, they will be notified through available channels and given 30 days to submit a valid address. If no valid address is received within 30 days, the compensation claim will be classified as invalid.

If Monocle receives fewer claims than affected trades, unclaimed funds will remain in Peer Security Reserve.

This process ensures transparency and accountability. Anyone will be able to verify all reimbursements independently using the on-chain information provided. No private information of peers will be published - this includes names, fiat account details, and Bitcoin or other cryptocurrency addresses.

Once trading resumes, Monocle will continue with the same model. 33% of network trade fees will continue being allocated to Community Reserves.

## Valuation and Reimbursement

All reimbursements are denominated in XMR. Where losses involve Bitcoin, fiat, or another asset, the XMR equivalent will be calculated at the rate on the date of the exploit.

In the event that a portion of Peer Security Reserve is denominated in Bitcoin, any Bitcoin used for reimbursements will first be converted to XMR. The transaction IDs for the conversion and the price used will be published alongside the compensation verification data.

## In Case of Insufficient Community Reserves

Monocle will cover losses **only up to the amount held in its reserves at the time the exploit happened**. This avoids accumulating obligations that would require network fee increases.

For example, if the total exploit is 1,000 XMR, and Monocle has only 500 XMR in its reserves, then Monocle will only cover 500 XMR of the 1,000 XMR exploit. Traders should take this into account when considering making or taking offers on the Monocle Network. Newly accumulated balances in the Community Reserves will not be used to cover for past exploits that received a partial refund.

Peers should understand that risks exist when using any Haveno network. While these risks may diminish over time, they are acknowledged as present.

Should the reserves not be enough to cover losses:

* Affected peers will receive proportional compensation based on total losses. For example, if reserve balances cover 50% of total losses, each affected peer would receive 50% of their verified loss. All amounts are denominated in XMR, valued at the rate on the date of the exploit.
* Upon resuming operations, network fees will remain unchanged. Exploits will not result in network fee increases.


## Accumulation of XMR in Peer Security Reserve

The reserves will keep increasing indefinitely if they don't need to be used. A portion of the Peer Security Reserve may be denominated in Bitcoin, given that the most likely affected trade pair in an exploit scenario would be XMR/BTC. Monocle will publish the bitcoin address where Peer Security Reserve is held.

Peer Security Reserve is allocated only for peers on the Monocle Network.

## In Case of Exploits Across Other Haveno Networks

Peer Security Reserve is exclusively dedicated to compensating peers who experienced losses on the Monocle Network, even when it would be enough to cover the damage of other networks.

## If Monocle Network is Retired

In the event that the network is retired for any reason, and no exploits have occurred requiring compensation, the remaining Peer Security Reserve balance will be retained by the network maintainers, with a portion donated to the Haveno project and the open-source ecosystem consistent with Monocle's values.
