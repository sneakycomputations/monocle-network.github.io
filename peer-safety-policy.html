Peer Safety Policy

Monocle recognizes that peer safety is the foundation of a trading network peers can trust. This Peer Safety Policy establishes Monocle Network's commitment to protecting peers in every aspect of the network: their funds, their trades, their privacy, and their recourse when something goes wrong.

Safety of peers is Monocle Network's top priority. Peer protection is built in layers: the Peer Security Reserve stands behind traders financially, policies define the rules trades are conducted and disputes are resolved under, and transparency makes every protection verifiable rather than taken on trust.

1. Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to:

  • Define the layers of protection that exist for traders, and how each one works
  • Promote a culture of safety, fairness, and accountability among contributors, peers and the wider community
  • Enhance trust and confidence in trading on the network, knowing that protections are defined in advance and verifiable after the fact

2. Safety Principles

The main principles of the Peer Safety Policy are outlined here:

  • Peer Safety First: The safety of peers takes priority over all other considerations, including network growth and revenue
  • Defined Protections: Every protection offered to traders is defined in advance, published, and never improvised after the fact
  • Fair Process: Disputes and appeals are handled by defined standards, applied impartially, with reasoning documented
  • Compensation Where Warranted: Peers affected by verified exploits are compensated from the Peer Security Reserve, on a published timeline, with every payout verifiable on-chain
  • Privacy is Protection: Peer privacy is non-negotiable - peer wallets, trades, and activity are private by design and are never published

3. Safety Practices

The following will guide Monocle Network's practices to ensure peer safety in all aspects of operations:

  • Incident Response: Respond to security incidents promptly and transparently, with peer protection as the top priority, following the defined response process
  • Reserve Management: Maintain and grow the Peer Security Reserve from network fees, so that funds are available when peers need them
  • Rule Enforcement: Apply the trading rules and table of penalties consistently, deterring bad actors and compensating affected traders
  • Continuous Improvement: Learn from incidents, disputes, and appeals, and update policies and practices to prevent recurrence

4. Safety Layers

Safety of peers is Monocle Network's top priority, and it is built in layers. The below describes the layers of protection that exist for traders, and how each one works for peers.

  • The primary protection: Peer Security Reserves - The Peer Security Reserve stands behind traders if the worst happens
  • Policies that protect peers when they are trading - Policies define how trades are conducted and disputes are resolved
  • Policies that protect traders outside the trade process - Additional policies define how fees are changed, responsible disclosures and incidents are handled, and Monocle's commitment to transparency
  • Transparency that protects traders - Transparency makes every protection verifiable. Don't trust, verify.

5. The primary protection: Peer Security Reserves

5.1 Peer Security Reserve

Monocle maintains a Peer Security Reserve, accrued from 33% of collected network fees, for one purpose: compensating peers affected by verified exploits. While Monocle is not directly liable for losses resulting from the underlying Haveno protocol, the reserve exists to mitigate exactly that kind of loss. In the event of an exploit, all available XMR across the Community Reserves is directed toward reimbursing affected peers - and if the reserves cannot cover every loss in full, peers are compensated proportionally, with every payout verifiable on-chain.

6. Policies that protect peers when they are trading

6.1 Dispute Resolution Policy

When a trade goes wrong and negotiation fails, the dispute resolution process ensures each case is heard by defined standards: arbitrators must respond within 24 hours, weigh evidence impartially, apply the published rules, and document their reasoning. A defined, transparent process protects traders far better than an arbitrary one.

6.2 Trading Rules

Every trade is conducted under published default rules: what is expected of buyers and sellers, how payment is made and confirmed, and what constitutes completion. Clear rules mean fewer disputes, and when a dispute does occur, the rules are the standard both peers agreed to.

6.3 Table of Penalties

Penalties for rule violations are defined in advance and published. When another peer breaks the rules - failing to pay, misleading the counterparty, wasting a peer's time - the penalty is already known, not improvised. Defined penalties deter bad actors and compensate affected traders.

6.4 Appeals Policy

Arbitrator decisions are not final. A peer who believes a decision in a dispute was incorrect can use the appeals process to have a different arbitrator review it and, where warranted, correct it. This protects traders from arbitrator error, and the deposit structure of appeals protects everyone from frivolous appeals.

7. Policies that protect traders outside the trade process

7.1 Fee Adjustment Policy

Fees can never be raised quietly or suddenly. Any change requires a published proposal, a minimum 30-day community discussion, a published decision, and a minimum 30-day notice period before it takes effect. This protects traders from surprise costs and gives them time to adjust or object.

7.2 Privacy Policy

Monocle collects no personal information, stores no data centrally, and asks no KYC. The privacy policy defines exactly what happens to the (very little) data that exists. For a trader, privacy is not a feature - it is protection for the peer and their funds.

7.3 Responsible Disclosure Policy

Security vulnerabilities exist in all software. This policy gives researchers a safe, defined channel to report them, compensates them for approved disclosures, and ensures fixes before problems are exploited. Vulnerabilities handled responsibly are vulnerabilities that never harm traders.

7.4 Transparency Policy

The transparency policy commits Monocle to publishing what it says it publishes: addresses, view keys, records, and reports. The policy makes transparency an obligation, not a gesture - and the community can hold the network to it.

7.5 Incident Response Policy

If an exploit occurs, the response is not improvised. The incident response policy defines the timeline: operations paused immediately, notification within 24 hours, losses assessed within 7 days, public disclosure within 14 days, and compensation distributions within a defined window. Traders affected by an incident know what will happen, when, and how they will be made whole.

8. Transparency that protects traders

8.1 Appeals Process Transparency

Every appeal outcome and its reasoning is published on GitHub, without identifying information. Traders can see how appeals are actually decided - building confidence in the process and holding arbitrators accountable for the quality of their reviews.

8.2 Canary

The warrant canary is a regularly published, PGP-signed statement affirming that Monocle has not been subject to legal process, secret orders, or coercion that would compromise the privacy of its peers. A dead canary - a missed update, a weakened statement - would tell traders something is wrong. Its continued publication is itself a protection.

8.3 Exploit Transparency

In the event of an exploit, everything is disclosed: total losses, affected trades, and every reimbursement, verifiable on-chain. Traders do not have to wonder what happened or whether they were compensated fairly - the record is public.

8.4 Peer Security Reserve Transparency

The reserve that stands behind traders is itself transparent: its addresses and view key are published, its balance is auditable by anyone, and its usage history shows every payout ever made. The primary protection for traders is open to inspection - its strength is verifiable, not asserted.

Don't trust, verify.
