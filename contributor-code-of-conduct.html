Contributor Code of Conduct

Contributors are the backbone of Monocle Network. They operate infrastructure, support peers, resolve disputes, and represent the network to the community. With this responsibility comes a higher standard of conduct.

This Code of Conduct applies to all compensated contributors, including support contributors, node operators, markets operator, arbitrators, and community steward. It applies in addition to the Community Guidelines - contributors are held to both standards.

1. Purpose

Contributors are trusted community members in positions that directly affect peer experience, network security, and community trust. This Code of Conduct establishes the professional and ethical standards expected of anyone representing Monocle Network in a compensated role.

The goal is not to constrain contributors unnecessarily, but to ensure that the trust peers place in Monocle is well-founded and that contributors understand the responsibilities that come with their roles.

2. Core Principles

All contributors are expected to uphold Monocle's core values in their conduct:

Value Contributor Application
Privacy Respect the privacy and pseudonymity of peers and fellow contributors at all times
Transparency Act openly in matters of network operations; never conceal information that the community deserves to know
Community Prioritize the well-being of the community over personal interest
Security Take every reasonable precaution to protect peers, the network, and sensitive information
Censorship resistance Never suppress legitimate community discussion or dissent

3. Professional Conduct

3.1 Accountability

  • Contributors are accountable for their actions in their Monocle capacity
  • Compensation requests must accurately reflect work completed - never inflate hours, misrepresent tasks, or claim compensation for work not done

3.2 Reliability

  • Contributors must meet the time commitments agreed for their role (e.g., minimum hours per week, response time expectations)
  • If a contributor is unable to meet their commitments, they must notify the Contributor Hostess promptly
  • Extended unavailability without notice may result in loss of the compensated role

3.3 Quality of Work

  • Contributors are expected to perform their duties competently and diligently
  • Contributors unfamiliar with specific aspects of their role should seek understanding or consult resources before acting
  • Contributors should actively seek feedback and improve their performance over time

4. Confidentiality

Contributors may have access to information that is not publicly available. Confidentiality obligations depend on the role:

4.1 All Contributors

All contributors must keep confidential:

  • Internal operational discussions among contributors
  • Security vulnerabilities before they have been publicly disclosed
  • Details of Monocle's infrastructure, including node locations, configurations, and security measures
  • Any information explicitly marked as confidential by other contributors

4.2 Arbitrators

Arbitrators have heightened confidentiality obligations, as detailed in the Dispute Resolution Policy:

  • Personal information and dispute details of peers involved in dispute resolution
  • Evidence provided during dispute resolution
  • Dispute resolution decisions and reasoning, which are shared only with the peers involved

4.3 Support Contributors

Support contributors may encounter:

  • Peer trade issues that contain personal or financial details
  • Reports of security incidents or exploits
  • Reports of community guideline violations

This information must not be disclosed beyond what is necessary to resolve the issue.

More information is detailed in the Support Policy.

4.4 What Is Not Confidential

The following are not confidential and must never be treated as such:

  • Network fee collection totals and reserve wallet balances. These are public by design
  • Contributor compensation requests and disbursements. These are published on GitHub
  • Community guideline violations that result in public moderation actions (e.g., a ban for harassment may be acknowledged)

5. Pseudonymity and OpSec

5.1 Maintaining Pseudonymity

Contributors operate under nyms. Contributors must:

  • Respect the pseudonymity of all other contributors. Do not attempt to identify, deanonymize, or link nyms to real-world identities
  • Maintain the separation between their Monocle nym and any other identities they hold
  • Not share their Monocle identity with or link it to identity-verified accounts on other platforms

5.2 Operational Security

Contributors must take reasonable precautions to protect their nym and Monocle-related activities:

  • Use Tor whenever possible for Monocle-related activities
  • Use unique, strong credentials for all Monocle-related accounts
  • Avoid discussing Monocle operational details on platforms or channels not designated for that purpose

5.3 No Identity Verification Required

Monocle does not require contributors to verify their real-world identity. A nym and proof of work are sufficient. However:

  • Contributors must not use their anonymity as cover for misconduct
  • Pseudonymity does not reduce accountability. Violations of this Code of Conduct carry the same consequences regardless of identity

6. Conflicts of Interest

Contributors must disclose any situation where their personal interests could conflict with their Monocle responsibilities:

6.1 What Constitutes a Conflict

  • Operating on or contributing to a competing Haveno network
  • Holding a financial position that could be affected by Monocle's operations or decisions
  • Any situation where a contributor could benefit personally from a decision they make in their Monocle capacity

6.2 How to Handle a Conflict

  • Disclose the conflict to the Contributor Hostess as soon as it becomes apparent. The Contributor Hostess must disclose any conflicts to the Support Contributors collectively
  • Recuse yourself from decisions or actions affected by the conflict
  • Do not use information gained through your Monocle role for personal advantage

6.3 Competing Networks

Contributors are not prohibited from participating in other Haveno networks. However:

  • Contributors must not use their Monocle role to recruit for, promote, or advantage a competing network
  • Contributors must not share Monocle's internal operational information with competing networks
  • Conflicts arising from dual participation must be disclosed to the Contributor Hostess

7. Handling Sensitive Information and Access

7.1 Wallet and Key Information

Some contributors may have access to sensitive operational information:

  • Node operators have access to infrastructure configurations
  • Support contributors may encounter peer trade details
  • Arbitrators have access to dispute information and may execute protocol-level actions

Contributors must:

  • Never share access credentials, private keys, or operational secrets with unauthorized parties
  • Never use their access for personal gain or to advantage any peer over another
  • Report any suspected unauthorized access or security breach to arbitrators immediately

7.2 Abuse of Access

Using access or authority gained through a contributor role for any purpose other than fulfilling that role's responsibilities is a serious violation. This includes, but is not limited to:

  • Requesting a peer to provide information beyond what is required for the role
  • Using arbitrator authority to influence trades or favour specific peers
  • Using infrastructure access to manipulate network operations
  • Withholding information that peers or the community need for transparency

8. Commitment to Transparency

Contributors are part of Monocle's commitment to radical transparency. This means:

  • Compensation requests must be honest, detailed, and posted publicly on GitHub
  • Decisions made in a contributor capacity should be explainable and justifiable if questioned
  • Contributors should not attempt to obscure, hide, or misrepresent information related to network operations
  • When in doubt about whether something should be public, lean toward transparency, or message the Contributor Hostess

9. Anti-Fraud and Anti-Exploitation

Contributors must never:

  • Submit fraudulent compensation requests
  • Collude with other contributors or peers to manipulate outcomes (e.g., trade manipulation, dispute resolution manipulation)
  • Exploit their position for financial gain beyond their agreed compensation
  • Use insider knowledge of network operations for personal trading advantage

Any form of fraud or exploitation is grounds for immediate termination of the contributor role and potential recovery of misappropriated funds.

10. Reporting Violations

10.1 Who Can Report

Anyone - peers, contributors, or community members - can report a violation of this Code of Conduct to the Contributor Hostess.

10.2 How to Report

  • SimpleX - Contact the Contributor Hostess, who will initiate a direct message for reporting.

Reports should include:

  • A description of the violation
  • When and where it occurred
  • Any relevant evidence
  • The nym of the contributor involved

10.3 Confidentiality of Reports

Reports will be treated confidentially. The identity of the reporter will not be disclosed without their consent.

10.4 No Retaliation

Retaliation against anyone who reports a violation in good faith is a violation of this Code of Conduct and will be treated as seriously as the original violation.

11. Consequences

Violations are assessed based on severity, intent, and impact. Consequences are applied regardless of the contributor's role, tenure, or status.

11.1 Response Framework

Response When Applied
Private discussion Minor first violation, likely unintentional
Formal warning Repeated minor violations or a more serious first offence
Suspension Serious violation requiring temporary removal from role while investigated
Termination of role Severe violations such as fraud, confidentiality breach, abuse of access, or persistent misconduct
Recovery of funds When compensation was obtained fraudulently or through misrepresentation

11.2 Process

  1. Report is received and reviewed by the Contributor Hostess not involved in the alleged violation
  2. The accused contributor is given an opportunity to respond
  3. The Contributor Hostess will assess the evidence and determine an appropriate response
  4. The outcome is communicated to the reporter and the accused contributor
  5. If the contributor's role is terminated, this is reflected in the public roles listing and compensation records

11.3 Appeals

Contributors may appeal a decision by contacting an arbitrator. Appeals must be submitted within 14 days of the decision.

12. Trial Period

New contributors serve a one-month trial period as described in the Get Involved page. During this period:

  • The contributor is not yet eligible for compensation
  • The Code of Conduct applies in full from the first day
  • Violations during the trial period may result in the contributor not progressing to a compensated role

13. Relationship to Other Policies

Policy Relationship
Community Guidelines Applies to all community members, including contributors. This Code of Conduct is additive
Dispute Resolution Policy Defines arbitrator-specific standards, which supplement this Code of Conduct
Bootstrapped Operations Policy Defines compensation processes and transparency requirements
Responsible Disclosure Policy Covers security vulnerability reporting; contributors who discover vulnerabilities should follow that policy

14. Changes to This Code of Conduct

This Code of Conduct may be updated periodically. Changes will be announced through public community channels and documented in version history. Substantive changes will be provided with at least 30 days' notice.

Contributions are held to high standards.
